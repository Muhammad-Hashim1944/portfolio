THE STRATEGIC PROBLEM

The Benefit-Scatter Plateau

Urapra already communicates several recognizable product benefits: uneven-looking tone, dullness, and rough or textured areas such as elbows, knees, and underarms. However, multiple creatives repeat similar benefit-led messaging with superficial hook swaps. The strategic opportunity is to move beyond swapping hooks and develop genuinely different creative concepts.

FROM — SCATTERED BENEFITS
“Here are many things glycolic acid can fix.”

Opens immediately with the bottle; generic on-screen benefit text; cramming multiple unrelated body areas into 30 seconds; leaves the viewer without a single relatable reason to buy.

TO — PROBLEM-LED ROUTINE
“Here is one relatable body-skin concern, why it may happen, and how this product fits into a sensible routine.”

Opens with natural friction-area recognition; explains surface dead-cell accumulation in everyday terms; establishes the 3% rinse-off body cleanser format; ends on a clear landing-page action.

THE SYSTEM UPGRADE

Key Strategic Improvements

Start with the problem: Hook the target buyer with a recognizable body tension in the first 1–2 seconds.

One problem per ad: Focus on elbows and knees rather than scattering focus across 4 body parts.

Simple glycolic explanation: Demystify it as an AHA that gently dissolves dead surface cells.

Match visuals to audio: Eliminate decorative filler B-roll; every cut reinforces the spoken line.

Clarify rinse-off format: Position as an everyday body wash cleanser, not a leave-on acid treatment.

Specific CTA: Direct visitors to review product directions on the exact 3% body wash page.

Honest expectations: Remove misleading before/after visuals in favor of truthful routine guidance.

EMPIRICAL ANALYSIS

What the 10-Ad Research Spreadsheet Reveals

Analyzing observations recorded across 10 active ads in the research spreadsheet to identify creative repetition and strategic opportunities.

Repeated Benefit-Led Messaging: Creatives consistently repeat the same generic claims (glow, smoothness, dark areas) rather than establishing distinctive, relatable consumer contexts.

Reused Footage with Changed Hooks: At least 3 ads in the research log recycle identical video footage with only the opening line modified, causing creative fatigue.

Concentration on Dark/Uneven Areas: High repetition around elbows, knees, and underarms without explaining friction causes or managing routine expectations.

Untapped Founder-Led Content: Strong opportunity to deploy founder or expert explainers to build trust around formulation and ingredient safety.

Educational & Informative Formats: Clear consumer whitespace for calm, educational videos explaining how AHAs function instead of aggressive direct-response claims.

Observation vs. Performance Boundary: The spreadsheet represents the strategist's qualitative observation log. Without ad account access, we do not claim which ads won or lost; rather, we diagnose creative patterns to engineer better testable variations.

AUDIENCE INSIGHTS

5 Core Audience Hypotheses

Tensions and consumer questions to validate through customer reviews, search queries, and comments—not assumed facts.

1. Uneven Tone on Elbows & Knees
“Mere elbows/knees baqi skin se zyada rough ya dull kyun lagte hain?”

Creative Opportunity: Lead with specific friction areas and explain dead cell buildup respectfully without shaming natural skin tone.

2. Rough or Dry-Feeling Texture
“Lotion lagane ke baad bhi skin smooth feel nahi hoti?”

Creative Opportunity: Explain that cleansing, exfoliating, and moisturising have distinct roles—body wash exfoliates surface dead cells before lotion hydrates.

3. Underarm Appearance Concerns
“Underarms ka tone uneven lagta hai—kya reason ho sakta hai?”

Creative Opportunity: Address friction, shaving, and irritation as contributors; avoid promising a universal cure.

4. Post-Hair-Removal Bumps
“Shaving ke baad skin rough ya bumpy feel hoti hai?”

Creative Opportunity: Separate educational angle addressing rough skin surface without claiming medical folliculitis cures.

5. Acid Confusion & Fear of Overuse
“Body wash mein glycolic acid ka kya kaam hai? Acid hai—kya roz use karna safe hai?”

Creative Opportunity: Demystify AHA exfoliation, explain gradual routine introduction, and emphasize rinse-off safety and sunscreen guidance.

Cosmetic vs. Medical Distinction: Body darkening in folds has diverse causes including friction, hair removal, and medical factors. A cosmetic body cleanser must never be framed as a medical diagnosis or guaranteed cure.

THE SYSTEM SOLUTION

Proposed Creative Strategy: “Body skincare ko bhi attention chahiye.”

A problem-aware educational video concept centered on rough, uneven-looking elbows and knees—framed as a helpful routine tip, not a hard-sell transformation.

Funnel Stage Problem-aware → Solution-aware (MOFU)
Primary Audience Pakistani shoppers seeking body skincare
Single Promise Gentle exfoliation supporting smoother skin
Desired Action Visit 3% product page & inspect directions

EDITORIAL STANDARDS

Editing Requirements

Strong first 1–2 seconds to halt social feeds instantly.

Clean, readable typography with strong contrast.

Premium, subtle motion graphics; no flashing strobe transitions.

Smooth, logical narrative flow connecting every spoken phrase to screen visuals.

Relevant context-driven B-roll only; cut filler vanity clips.

No distracting stickers, emojis, or loud sound effects.

No excessive subtitle clutter; let the visuals breathe.

Maintain consistent pacing across all 34 seconds.

Ensure the “3% Glycolic Acid Body Wash” label remains crisp and readable.

COMPLIANCE & SAFETY

Creator Shoot Instructions

MANDATORY DOS
  • Record all 3 hooks separately with identical lighting & framing.
  • Keep the same body and CTA for controlled testing variations.
  • Follow official brand safety directions and sun protection guidance.
STRICT DON'TS
  • Do NOT use beauty filters or skin-lightening grading.

ROOT-CAUSE ANALYSIS

Creative Performance Diagnosis Matrix

Weak 3s hold / early drop

The hook line or first-frame visual is failing to stop the scroll in the feed.

Next Action: Test a new hook take and opening visual first. Keep the body and CTA unchanged.
Sharp drop during explanation

The educational explanation is slow, confusing, or disconnected from the initial hook promise.

Next Action: Tighten the script, remove filler lines, and ensure visuals show the body area while speaking.
Good watch time, low outbound CTR

The product bridge, offer relevance, or call-to-action is weak; viewers feel informed but unmotivated to click.

Next Action: Test a clearer product relevance bridge and make the next landing page step more urgent.
Good CTR, low landing-page views

Slow site load times, tracking drop-offs, accidental clicks, or redirect friction.

Next Action: Flag immediately to the media buyer and developer to audit page speed and tracking tags.
Good LP views, low add-to-cart

Mismatch between ad creative and product page, insufficient proof, or unclear usage directions.

Next Action: Audit ad-to-page promise continuity and review product page clarity with the store team.
High add-to-cart, low purchases

Checkout friction: surprise shipping fees, missing payment methods, trust concerns, or checkout technical bugs.

Next Action: Escalate directly to the ecommerce store owner; this is a checkout issue, not a script issue.

Strategic Media Buying Principle: “Do not declare a winner based on 3-second hold alone. Evaluate enough spend and conversion volume for a fair comparison.”
