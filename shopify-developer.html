HM Ismail & Sons sells bathroom, sanitary ware, and hardware fittings out of Muslim Bazaar in Sargodha. The founder, Ahmad, had already built a loyal customer base through word of mouth and local trust, but his online store didn't carry that same weight. It felt cluttered and generic, the kind of site that could belong to almost any hardware seller in the country.

Ahmad needed something simpler. A minimal, professional Shopify store that let his products speak for themselves instead of competing with banners and pop-ups for attention.

The results showed up fast. In the month after the changes went live, HM Ismail & Sons brought in PKR 158,473 in total sales, a 440 percent increase over the previous month.

Sands Drop started with nothing. No existing customer base, no catalogue, no infrastructure of any kind. It was a UAE-targeted dropshipping store, launched and managed entirely from Pakistan.

The work covered everything from the ground up, from listing and optimizing the full product catalogue to setting up the Shopify apps the store needed to keep sales moving.

The full month of July 2025 shows what that built. The store pulled in 8,460 sessions, a 129 percent increase. Gross sales reached AED 31,452, up 315 percent, and orders climbed to 329, a 345 percent jump.

trysoreva.com: Testing at Scale to Find What Sells

trysoreva.com runs on volume. Every week, new products get pulled from research tools, listed, and pushed into ads to see what the market actually wants. Most get cut within days, which keeps ad spend away from products that were never going to sell.

Not every test lands. One product tested between November 9 and December 9, 2025 brought in 830 sessions, a 40 percent drop from the month before, along with a 76 percent fall in sales and a conversion rate down to 1.57 percent.

Then there's the one that worked. Over a longer window, from September 10 to December 9, 2025, sessions climbed to 3,830, a 677 percent increase. That test brought in $3,301 across 84 orders, at a 2.06 percent conversion rate.

The two results next to each other make the point better than either one alone. Listing fast and cutting what doesn't perform is what makes room for the product that does.
